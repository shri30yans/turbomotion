The audit: where the energy shortfall came from (evidence)
- The engine's one blur was `Layer::blurred`, Skia's image filter: a Gaussian sampled out to 3 sigma, exact only to about sigma 4. Above that Skia works on a reduced-resolution copy, so its light depends on sigma **and on the picture's size** and it both loses and invents light. Light of a lone pixel after `blur` (1.000 is right), measured by `the_legacy_blur_counts_the_light_of_a_small_source_by_its_sigma_and_by_the_size_of_the_picture`: on a 161 px picture sigma 4 gives 1.000, 5 gives 0.879, 6 gives 0.56 to 0.84, 8 and above give 0.000; on 256 px sigma 5 gives 1.20, 6 gives 1.27, 8 gives 1.00, 12 gives 1.27, 16 gives 1.00, 24 gives 1.27. On rendered 8-bit frames a 4 x 4 square returns 1.0016, 1.0307, 0.8800, 0.9997, 1.2629 at sigma 4, 5, 6, 8, 12 (`probes/data.tsv`); a larger square (6 x 6 at sigma 12) is within 0.1%, so the error belongs to small sources, which is exactly what a halation halo is built from.
- Not from the edge: a half-plane is within 0.92 levels (display film) and 3.26 levels (linear light) of the error function in the legacy blur, so edges hid it. A point source's halation peak is 0.9817, 0.9802, 0.9802 of the analytic Gaussian at sigma 16, 32, 64; a 64 px square at sigma 24 is 2.5% low at the centre. `glow`, `bloom`, `shadow`, `film_halation`, `backdrop`, `directional_blur`, `radial_blur` and the `tilt_shift` plugin were all built on, or beside, that blur. `tilt_shift` had a second, hand-written one (16 spiral taps).
- `radial_blur` and `directional_blur` were sampled row-of-copies shaders (24 and up to 64 taps) with no spin; the reach used for growth (`3 * sigma`) was also not what an exact kernel reads.

The design (kernel + passes + precision)
- **One mechanism**, `kernel::Blur { shape, edge }`. Gaussian, box and iris are shapes; edge is `Transparent` (light leaves, the layer grows) or `Repeat` (the edge pixels continue, the layer does not grow). Glow, shadow, bloom-adjacent halation, backdrop, the plugins' kernel passes and the smears all call `Blur::apply`/`Smear::apply`. No second blur sits beside Skia's: Skia's is the legacy path only, kept bit for bit.
- **Passes.** Gaussian and box: two separable passes (`separable.sksl`), each a weighted mean over `2 * taps + 1` pixels, reading 4 sigma each side (`taps::REACH`; what is left, 6.3e-5 of the weight, is under 0.01 of an 8-bit level on an edge). A box repeats `iterations` times and has a fractional last tap, so its radius animates without steps. Iris (Camera Lens Blur): one pass over the iris's pixels (`iris.sksl`), weight = coverage by the disc or polygon (one pixel of linear falloff), so the disc is flat-topped. Smear (directional, zoom, spin): one pass taking a sample per pixel of path along a line, a ray or a circle (`smear.sksl`). Cost is linear in sigma, so there is **no mip pyramid** (see Deviations).
- **Precision.** Weights and sums are 32-bit float, and the mean divides by the weights it actually read, so they add to exactly 1 at every sigma and a pixel past the edge is lost light or a repeated pixel, never a smaller kernel. Pictures between passes are half-float like every picture; a smooth 256-step gradient stays monotone with no step larger than the ideal.
- **Limits are planner errors, never silent.** Sigma above 512 (`MAX_TAPS` 2048), an iris radius of 32 or more (`MAX_IRIS_TAPS`), more than 16 iterations or blades, a smear longer than 2048 samples at its worst pixel, and a spin on `quality: legacy` are refused when the film is built (`Effect::check`, using the largest value each animated parameter takes), with the limit in the message. The Rust constants and the shader loop bounds are written from one source (`taps.rs`).
- **The opt-in.** A per-effect `quality: legacy | exact` choice, default `legacy`, on `blur`, `glow`, `shadow`, `film_halation`, `backdrop`, `directional_blur`, `radial_blur` and the `tilt_shift` plugin. I chose a parameter on the effect, and not a project-level switch, because the effect-space and colour pipeline switches already exist at project level and a film-wide flag would flip the look of every effect at once, including ones the author did not look at; per effect a film can move one glow at a time and the stored JSON says which blur it means. It is versioned by being explicit: a film with no `quality` renders exactly as before (proved below). New effects `box_blur`, `lens_blur` and `unsharp_mask` have no legacy form and are kernel only. `blur` also gained `dimensions` and `edge`, which both qualities honour (legacy through Skia's per-axis sigma and tile mode, so `{"effect":"blur","radius":8}` is untouched); `radial_blur` gained `mode: zoom | spin` (spin needs `exact`). Plugins say `@pass name kernel=gaussian|box radius=<expr> [from=] [edge=] [iterations=]`; a choice is its option's number in an expression, so `radius=quality*blur/2` is no blur under `legacy`, which is how `tilt_shift` keeps its old look under its first option.
- **Opt-in effects that get the new kernel:** `blur`, `box_blur`, `lens_blur`, `unsharp_mask` (kernel), `glow`, `shadow`, `film_halation`, `backdrop`, `directional_blur`, `radial_blur` (zoom and spin), `tilt_shift`. `bloom` and the shutter/post-processing blur are not converted.

Deviations from this plan:
- **No mip pyramid above radius 32 (step 1).** The cost is linear and about 17 ms at sigma 64 at 1080p, so a pyramid would add an approximation (and the 0.5% energy and 3% sigma criteria) for a time that does not need it. Sigma above 512 is refused rather than approximated. If a wider blur is ever wanted, a pyramid is a new kernel shape, not a change to this one.
- **Not built (step 3):** Channel Blur, Cross Blur, Smart Blur, Bilateral Blur, Compound Blur (map layer) and Sharpen. Unsharp Mask is built. Cross Blur is not a row of the ledger. They remain `todo` (and `part` for Sharpen, whose plugin is untouched) in `parity/effects.tsv`, and `Bilateral Blur`, `Channel Blur`, `Compound Blur`, `Smart Blur`, `Reduce Interlace Flicker` are still the WP-02 gaps `parity gaps` lists.
- **Fast Box Blur** is a row sharing `box_blur` (a box with iterations), `part`: it has no spec or probe of its own. **Camera Lens Blur** is `part`: the iris (blades, rotation, roundness) is built, AE's highlight gain/threshold/saturation, blur map, aspect and fringe are not. **Directional Blur** and **Radial Blur** are `part` (no AE parameter mapping; the `ae_params` column is still `-`). **Gaussian Blur, Box Blur and Unsharp Mask** are `done`.
- **No plugin `disc` kernel pass (step 2).** Plugins can declare `gaussian` and `box`; the disc is an effect (`lens_blur`) and nothing in the current plugins needs it as a pass.
- **`bloom` is not converted.** It needs a conditional pass chain (threshold, then several widths), which the kernel pass grammar does not express and the plan does not ask for; it stays on Skia. `post.rs` and the shutter's blur are untouched.
- **`tilt_shift` is an approximation under `exact`.** It rewrites the original 16-spiral-tap shader as three kernel passes blended by the band, so the rows in the band are sharp and the far rows are a true Gaussian, but the rows between are a blend of two Gaussians, not a Gaussian of intermediate sigma (`exact_tilt_shift_keeps_its_band_sharp_...` asserts the far rows against an exact `blur` within 1 level). It keeps its name and parameters, and its default is `legacy`.
- **The default is still `legacy`**, so no existing film changes and the plan's "corrected blur" is opt-in; flipping it is the owner's decision (see questions). The plan also says "the plan's 4% low" for a halation halo: I measured **1.8% to 2.0%** for a point source (0.9817, 0.9802, 0.9802), and 2.5 to 3% at the centre of a 64 px square, so the numbers in `film_halation.md`, the doc comments and the new test say that, not 4%.
- **`sigma = blurriness / 2` is not verified.** There is no After Effects dump; `parity/specs/gaussian_blur.md` has the reasoning, marked UNSURE, and the SDK does not convert (`radius` is the sigma).
- **8-bit limits of the probe tables.** A 4 x 4 square at sigma 16 and 32 reads 0.9895 and 0.9581 in the exact column because the tail is under half a level; the unit tests (floats) hold 0.5%. A linear-light 8-bit edge at sigma 2 reaches 1.43 levels (discretisation of the closed form plus quantisation), so the render tests use sigma 4 to 24; the float edge test (`kernel/tests.rs`) covers sigma 2 to 32 within 1 level (0.67 at sigma 2, the midpoint rule's error).
- `kernel/rig.rs` helpers are `#[cfg(test)]` in the engine crate rather than a shared test crate, as the other core test rigs are.
