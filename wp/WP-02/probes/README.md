Generated by `make.py` (`python3 wp/WP-02/probes/make.py`), which writes the five probe films to `parity/probes/`, renders each with `comp still`, and measures 8-bit stills of the same films with other sigmas into `data.tsv` (`probe quantity setting legacy exact expected`). Every probe film passes `comp check` with 0 errors and 0 warnings. The images are only there to confirm what the numbers say; the figures are measured on pixels, and the float-precision claims are in the unit tests named in `parity/specs/`.

Two limits of an 8-bit still, so the table is not misread: a level of 255 cannot hold a tail under half a level (a 4 x 4 square at sigma 16 and 32 reads 0.9895 and 0.9581 of its light in the exact column, the missing light being the tail below one level; the unit tests, which read floats, hold the same blur to 0.5%), and a flat plateau rounds to one level (a disc of 23.5 levels shows 23).

- `gaussian_blur.json`, `gaussian_blur.png`: a white half-plane blurred by sigma 16 in three bands, top to bottom: legacy, exact, exact with `dimensions: horizontal` (the top and bottom of that band stay crisp); below, a 4 x 4 square at sigma 8, legacy left, exact right. A hard edge is the error function to within **0.65, 0.46, 0.51, 0.55 levels** (display film) and **0.73, 0.52, 0.55, 0.56 levels** (linear-light film) at sigma 4, 8, 16, 24 for the exact blur (asserted at 1 level); the legacy blur is at **0.83 to 0.92 levels** (display) and **2.77 to 3.26 levels** (linear light) on the same edges. A half-plane hides what the legacy blur gets wrong; a small source shows it: the light of a 4 x 4 square after the blur, legacy against exact (1.0000 is right):

| sigma | legacy | exact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 1.0016 | 1.0010 |
| 5 | 1.0307 | 0.9992 |
| 6 | 0.8800 | 0.9982 |
| 8 | 0.9997 | 0.9982 |
| 12 | 1.2629 | 0.9950 |
| 16 | 0.9934 | 0.9895 (8-bit tail) |

  With a square that grows with the blur (6 x 6 at sigma 12, 8 x 8 at 16, 16 x 16 at 32) both are within 0.3%, which is the plan's point: the legacy error is a property of small sources and of the picture's size, and it is largest where a halo is built from a point.
- `box_blur.json`, `box_blur.png`: a half-plane through a box of radius 6 once (top) and three times (middle), and an 8 x 8 square (lower left). One iteration is the linear ramp `clamp((d + 6.5) / 13)` to **0.4 levels** (asserted at 1); three iterations are **10.6 levels** from a Gaussian of the same variance (sigma 6.48), so "near a Gaussian", not one. The 8 x 8 square returns **0.9988** of its light and lights **20 px** (8 + 2 * 6), as it should.
- `lens_blur.json`, `lens_blur.png`: a 6 x 6 square through an iris of radius 28 as a disc (left), a hexagon (middle) and a pentagon (right), each a flat-topped shape of about 32 levels (36 / (pi 28^2) of light, encoded). On the 2 x 2 square and the radius 4, 6, 8, 12 discs of `data.tsv` the plateau is flat (0 levels of spread over the middle 70% of the radius), no light lies past radius + 2 px, and the level is **80, 53, 39, 23** against the closed form `encode(4 / (pi r^2))` = 79.7, 52.8, 38.6, 23.5.
- `unsharp_mask.json`, `unsharp_mask.png`: an edge from level 100 to level 160 at amount 100, sigma 4. The result is within **0.6, 0.53, 0.65 levels** of `v + a (v - blur)` (sigma 2, 4 at 100%; sigma 4 at 200%), asserted at 1; a flat grey of 100 at amount 300 changes **0** pixels.
- `radial_blur.json`, `radial_blur.png`: three 6 x 6 squares spun by 30 degrees at 100, 200, 300 px right of the centre (arcs, not lines) and two zoomed by 0.3 at 100 and 200 px above it (rays). In `data.tsv`, a 4 x 4 square at 200 px spun by 20 degrees spreads **4.9 px** in radius (the source's own 4 px plus the sampling), **21.0 degrees** in angle (20 plus the square's 1.1 degrees, less the faint ends) and keeps **1.004** of its light; at 60 degrees, **4.9 px, 60.9 degrees, 1.011**.
